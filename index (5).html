Interactive narrative + research

Surviving the Border

A branching mobile experience using story, player choice, consequence, multimedia, and research to help participants consider an unfamiliar perspective.

Narrative designInteractionResearchExperience design
Read the case study

Role

Co-designer and research collaborator with Begum Sacak, built in the ARIS mobile game platform.

The design challenge

Could a mobile game use choice and consequence to help players consider a difficult real-world journey from a perspective unlike their own?

The experience

Players moved through decisions and consequences supported by sound, imagery, video, location, and narrative context. The structure made tradeoffs visible while asking players to remain inside the story.

Research

Seventy-two percent of participants reported increased empathy for Latin American immigrants after the experience. Interviews and questionnaires also indicated greater awareness and emotional response. These were participant-reported findings, not a measure of interface usability or proof of lasting behavior change.

What it demonstrates

Branching narrative, decision architecture, experiential design, research collaboration, multimedia storytelling, and the challenge of simplifying a complex real-world issue without losing the human context.

Decision-flow artifact

A branching map from the original experience showing how early choices, reactions, hazards, and consequences converged toward the border-crossing outcome.

Interactive systems + gamification

Pokémon: Global and Local Cultures

Three interconnected user paths, progression mechanics, meaningful choices, rewards, and navigation designed inside the constraints of an LMS.

Systems designChoice architectureGamificationInformation architecture
Read the case study

Role

Collaborative instructional design with Nina Prozzo, faculty member Dr. Mark Peterson, and student graphic designers at Miami University.

The design challenge

A semester-long face-to-face anthropology course needed to become a condensed online experience that supported both majors and non-majors while preserving accessibility and meaningful choice.

The system

Students selected one of three character paths. A linked structure connected each path to assignments and the gradebook, while points, levels, resources, optional modules, and creative assessment choices created visible progression.

What it demonstrates

Branching systems, progress cues, alternate paths, motivation, information architecture, and designing an engaging experience within a constrained platform.

Surviving the Border

A mobile serious game co-designed with Begum Sacak on the ARIS platform. Players experience a choice-driven journey from the perspective of a fictional 17-year-old Honduran facing threats of violence and attempting to cross the U.S. border. Sound, imagery, video, and consequential choices supported the experience.

The research explored whether perspective-taking through play could affect empathy, knowledge, and emotional response. In post-game research, 72% of participants reported increased empathy toward Latin American immigrants.

Source artifacts: ARIS mobile screens, choice-based journey, and research findings.

GamificationLXDAccessibility

Pokémon: Global and Local Cultures

A six-week online Anthropology course redesigned from a face-to-face semester course. Learners chose among three character-based learning paths, earned points and resources as they progressed, and could submit creative or written projects. The experience replaced major exams with a Pecha Kucha and book-review project and was designed to be accessible.

Source artifacts: Kenshusei milestone artwork, character paths, course structure, and reward design.
03
EdTechCourse architectureUX

Managerial Accounting

Online course architecture for a high-enrollment, state-regulated course delivered in three- and six-week formats. Modular structure, short purposeful video lectures, optional practice, frequent feedback, and carefully timed optional live sessions helped learners navigate a dense curriculum, including students studying abroad.

Source artifacts: Course screens, module structure, video/handout patterns, and learner workflow.

Active & Adaptive Learning

At Arizona State University, I supported faculty and the adoption of active and adaptive learning approaches. The work included complex course-development initiatives, platform research, faculty support, and the design of learning frameworks across large-enrollment programs.

Documented outcomes: 10–20% increase in student success; 10K+ learners impacted across 115 sections and five departments.

Accessible Learning & Digital Communication

Work across higher education included accessible course reviews, faculty training, document remediation, infographics, course graphics, event flyers, Canvas badges, and a WordPress Office of Student Success site. I approach accessibility as part of the design process, alongside information hierarchy and usability.

Source artifacts: Canvas badges, learning-design flyers, course graphics, and training materials.

Holmes Health Case Study

An original six-page concept for a healthcare course, using a fictional healthcare system as an overarching narrative. The case study was designed to connect course activities through a shared scenario rather than treating each activity as an isolated exercise.

Source artifacts: Six-page case-study overview and scenario-based learning concept.

How I work

Make it meaningful.
Make it usable.

I connect learner and user needs, clear information structures, narrative, and technology—then use evidence and iteration to improve the experience.

01 / Understand

Start with people

Use research, user context, learner needs, and constraints to define the problem before choosing a format.

02 / Structure

Shape the journey

Design content flow, choices, feedback, and navigation so people can understand where they are and what comes next.

03 / Evaluate

Make the impact visible

Bring accessibility, evaluation, and iteration into the work rather than treating them as final checks.

About me

A researcher’s questions.
A storyteller’s instincts.

My experience spans learning experience design, educational technology, faculty development, student success, course and curriculum design, accessibility, and user research. I’ve led cross-functional course-development teams, supported educators, designed digital and print communication, and worked on a research-informed serious game.

I’m especially interested in roles where interaction and story can make a complex system easier to understand—whether that system is a learning product, a digital interface, or a narrative experience. My work balances creative thinking with evidence, accessibility, and respect for the people using the experience.
